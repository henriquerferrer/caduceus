# F1.10 — Verify refuses out-of-range code lengths, and takes every time from the store clock

Twilio refuses a `CustomCode` (start) or `Code` (check) that is not 4 to 10
characters long with error 60200 (HTTP 400): see
https://www.twilio.com/docs/verify/api/verification and
https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/60200. Caduceus captures a short
`CustomCode` and counts a short check as a wrong attempt. While in the same
handlers, this item also makes them take every timestamp from the store's
injectable clock, and fixes the README's `otp` wording for Verify captures.

## Spec
- **Surface:** `internal/server/verify.go`, `internal/server/server.go`
  (`handleExpireVerification`), `internal/server/server_test.go`, `README.md`.
- **Interfaces:** a code length outside 4–10 answers the existing Twilio error
  writer with 60200 and HTTP 400. The handlers read time through the store's
  clock (the one `Store.SetClock` sets), so the expire hook's `ExpiresAt` and
  each send attempt's `Time` come from it instead of `time.Now()`.
- **Out of scope:** checking what characters a code contains (only the length
  is checked; Twilio's alphabet rules are not emulated); Verify service
  settings such as a configured code length.

## Acceptance
- (server test) a start with `CustomCode` of 3 or 11 characters answers 60200
  (HTTP 400), creates no verification and captures no message; 4 and 10
  characters are accepted and captured.
- (server test) a check with a `Code` of 3 or 11 characters answers 60200
  (HTTP 400) and does not use up a check attempt: the verification's attempt
  count is unchanged; after 5 wrong checks of valid length, the 6th answers
  60202 as before.
- (server test) with an injected store clock, the expire hook's `expires_at`
  and a send attempt's `time` equal the injected time.
- (README) the Verify section states the 4–10 length rule, and the
  `/api/v1/wait` text says that a Verify capture's `otp` is the verification's
  code, while other messages' `otp` is the first 4 to 8 digit code in the body
  (F1 audit finding `### 6`).
